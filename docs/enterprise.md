# SpaceAI for companies and partners

**English** · [Русский](enterprise.ru.md) · [Back to repository](../README.md)

**SpaceAI is a unified control layer for enterprise AI agents.** Our focus is where an agent's output becomes an action in a business system: reading records, creating tickets, changing objects or calling internal APIs.

When multiple agents connect to critical systems, teams need to answer: who can act, which operations are allowed, when a person must approve, and what evidence remains afterward?

## Start with your workflow

| Your situation | What to discuss with SpaceAI |
| --- | --- |
| Agents are moving from a pilot into CRM, ERP, helpdesk or internal APIs | Access boundaries, controlled execution and audit requirements |
| Your B2B SaaS customers want agents to use your product | A consistent, governed integration layer for agent access |
| Several agent workflows span a complex or legacy IT landscape | Shared policies and integration patterns across systems |
| Security review is blocking an agent rollout | A reviewable workflow, explicit permissions and agreed evidence for approval |

The strongest fit is a mid-sized or large organization with an IT, security, architecture or platform team and a meaningful cost of agent mistakes.

## This repository and the broader platform

| Scope | What to evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| **Published here: Guard prototype** | Local harness sandboxing, environment controls, egress policy, content rules and filter decision logs. See [implementation limits](usage.md#limitations). |
| **Broader SpaceAI deployment discussion** | Enterprise access governance, system connectors, risk-based approvals, controlled action execution, centralized audit and operational rollout. Availability and scope are agreed for the specific deployment. |

The Guard source is one component. It does not contain a complete enterprise control plane, connector catalog, RBAC/ABAC system, approval service, management console or SIEM integration.

## A practical pilot conversation

1. Pick one business workflow and one or two target systems.
2. Map agents, operations, data boundaries and owners.
3. Agree which actions are allowed, blocked or require human approval.
4. Define technical and business acceptance criteria, including evidence for IT and security review.
5. Confirm integration scope, deployment model and the next rollout stage.

Pilot scope, delivery dates, commercial terms and support are agreed after reviewing the environment. No fixed outcome or rollout duration is implied by the public prototype.
